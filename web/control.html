Phone control

The ESP32-S3 makes its own Wi-Fi network and serves one page. Join the network with any phone, open the page, and fly: a throttle that always ramps smoothly, a direction wheel that moves the right vanes, yaw, arm and kill. The phone on the left is the real page running against a simulated vehicle; try it.

Demo · keys: arrows, W/S, A/D, Enter, SpaceFull screen →

Connect in four steps

  1. Power the vehicle with the propeller off. The ESP32-S3 starts the network MC1-FLY (password in firmware/monocopter/include/config.h; change it).
  2. Join MC1-FLY on your phone. If it says "no internet", choose stay connected.
  3. Open http://192.168.4.1/ in the phone's browser (type http://, not https://).
  4. The link light turns green. Move the wheel: the vanes follow while the vehicle is disarmed, so you can check every direction before the propeller goes on.

What each control does

ControlWhat it does
Throttle trackDrag to set the target. The motor follows through a ramp (0.30 per second up, 0.60 per second down), so a jump on the slider is never a jump on the motor. A thin line shows the real motor value.
▲ Climb / ▼ DescendHold to raise or lower the target at a steady rate: Smooth 6, Normal 12, Quick 24 percent per second.
HoverSets the target to the hover throttle (45 percent by default, tuned on the tether).
LandBrings the throttle down slowly (8 percent per second), then tells you to disarm.
Direction wheelPush toward the way you want the vehicle to tilt and move. Release and it springs back to the centre. The sensitivity is in Settings.
YawSlide left or right to turn; it springs back to the centre.
Hold to armHold for 0.9 s with the throttle at zero. Tap to disarm.
KillThe motor stops at once. The vehicle stays off until you arm it again.
Throttle limitStarts at 50 percent every time the page opens. Raise it in steps on the tether (Settings).

Which servo moves for which direction

The vanes work below the centre of gravity, so a side force at the vanes tilts the thrust line the opposite way round. The firmware does that for you; the table is what to expect when you check the vanes on the bench.

Safety, enforced in the firmware

  • It arms only on a fresh press of ARM, with the throttle at zero, after ARM was seen off since power-up.
  • No commands for 0.4 s: the throttle ramps down with the vanes centred. No commands for 2.5 s: disarmed. It never re-arms by itself.
  • The throttle limit and the ramps apply in the firmware, not only in the page.
  • Fly tethered. Wi-Fi is not a radio-control link: keep the phone close, the screen on, and expect the occasional dropout.
